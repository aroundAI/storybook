## KB-137 — Opening a social post can fail with "Social post not found" when the database is busy

> **Fixed (2026-09-28), #453.** `20260928045249_kb137-policy-auth-uid-once.sql`
> rewrites the fifteen policies with `(select auth.uid())` in place of
> `auth.uid()`, and nothing else: each statement is the live policy text with
> that one substitution. The same read of one post went from 4.0 s to 1.4 ms
> in `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`, and from 4.1–4.5 s to 3–27 ms over HTTP.
> `kb137-policy-auth-uid-once.test.sql` fails on any policy that filters
> through accounts or accounts_memberships with a bare `auth.uid()` (it
> reported 15 before the migration), and checks that a member still reads and
> edits the team's post while a stranger does not.
>
> **Also KB-128**, filed three days earlier for the same read and cause
> (9 s on 735 accounts); closed as fixed by this PR in the 2026-09-29 records
> audit.

**Severity:** Medium. It is a function of how many accounts exist in the whole
database, not in the user's team: with one account in production today it is
fast, and it gets slower for everyone with every account added. At 592 accounts
one read took half the timeout. **Found:** 2026-09-28, following up
`action-refusals-evidence.spec.ts › approving a post whose text another tab
cleared`, which failed in #452's local-CI evidence stage (lead 6 of the
2026-09-27 wave).

### What happened

The page showed no "Approve & Publish" button, and on the retry the click
toasted "Failed to publish Social post not found". Kong logged the reads of
`social_posts` as **HTTP 500**, and PostgREST logged
`57014 canceling statement due to statement timeout`, cancelled inside
`is_team_member`, `is_account_team_member` and `is_aal2`: the `accounts`
table's own policy functions.

`social_posts_select` is `account_id IN (SELECT id FROM accounts WHERE id =
auth.uid() UNION ALL SELECT account_id FROM accounts_memberships WHERE user_id =
auth.uid())`. Inlined, `auth.uid()` is a text-to-uuid cast that is not
leakproof, so Postgres applies it only after the subquery table's policies.
The plan was a sequential scan of `accounts` running `is_mfa_compliant()`,
`has_role_on_account()` and `is_account_team_member()` on all 592 rows: 540,169
buffer hits and 3,995 ms, as the post's owner, on an idle database. A busy
local-CI run pushed it past the authenticated role's 8 s `statement_timeout`.
With `(select auth.uid())` the comparison is an InitPlan parameter, the scan is
`accounts_pkey`, and the policies run on the one matching row.

The same shape was on `account_oauth_apps`, `batch_generation_jobs` and
`project_templates`: fifteen policies in all.

### Not fixed here

- 63 other policies use a bare `auth.uid()` against their own table's columns.
  That costs one cheap comparison per row of that table rather than a policy
  evaluation per account, so it was left alone.
- The action showed a timeout as "not found": KB-138.

### Acceptance criteria

- [x] No policy filters through accounts or accounts_memberships with a bare
  `auth.uid()` (pgTAP, 15 → 0)
- [x] A member still reads and edits the team's post, a stranger still does not
- [x] Reading one post as its owner at 592 accounts: 4.0 s → 1.4 ms
