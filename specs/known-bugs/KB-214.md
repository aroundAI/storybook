## KB-214 — A scheduled YouTube video went public before YouTube had made its HD version, so launch viewers got 360p

> **Fixed (2026-10-22), #693.** A YouTube post never goes public before
> YouTube has made it HD.
>
> **Staged ahead of its time.** Each 5-minute run, the scheduled-publish
> Lambda (`apps/web/lambda/scheduled-publish/index.ts`,
> `stageUpcomingYouTube`) sends to the worker every YouTube post (long video
> or Short) due within its project's lead time, `projects.metadata.youtubeLeadMinutes`
> (default 120, 0 turns it off; `shouldStage` in
> `packages/features/publishing/src/lib/youtube-staging.ts`), but not
> within two minutes; deleted episodes' posts (KB-207) and Meta-paused
> connections (KB-213) are left out, and the cadence rules are asked as at
> the post's time. The worker uploads it private with `status.publishAt` at
> its slot, sets the long video's thumbnail (KB-210), refuses a video over
> 15 minutes for a channel not verified for long uploads (KB-205) before a
> byte goes, and puts the row back to `scheduled` with
> `platform_content_id`, `platform_url` and `metadata.youtube = {videoId,
> stagedAt, publishAt, delays: []}`. The row is claimed (`queued`, then
> `publishing` with KB-206's `publishingStartedAt`) while it uploads, and
> both due-row queries (the Lambda's and the in-process job's) skip a row
> with `metadata.youtube`, so nothing sends it twice. A staged video counts
> as posted for KB-213's duplicate guard.
>
> **The HD gate.** The run also asks the worker to check each channel with
> a staged upload (`checkStagedYouTube`,
> `packages/features/publishing/src/lib/youtube-staged-check.ts`; one
> `videos.list part=status,processingDetails,contentDetails` per 50 videos).
> A video whose `publishAt` is within ten minutes and is not
> `contentDetails.definition === 'hd'` with
> `processingDetails.processingStatus === 'succeeded'` (or a Short whose
> long video is not public yet) has its `publishAt` moved ten minutes past
> the later of that time and now, on YouTube (`videos.update part=status`,
> the status sent back whole: audience, AI label, licence, embedding) and
> in `scheduled_at`, so the Schedule tab, the cadence and the Shorts queue
> see the time it will go; each hold is pushed to `metadata.youtube.delays`
> as `{at, from, to, reason}` (`hd_not_ready`, `processing`,
> `waiting_for_long_video`). It repeats every run until YouTube has HD, so a
> video goes public within about ten minutes of it. After six hours of
> holds in all the row carries `metadata.youtube.alert = 'held_over_6h'`
> and is still held. A rejected or abandoned upload fails the row at once
> with KB-205's words; a video YouTube made public marks the row
> `published` at its publish time; one deleted on YouTube fails it with
> "Deleted on YouTube before going public."
>
> **A breaking release waits for HD too** (owner, 2026-10-22). Its YouTube
> post, when due, uploads private with no `publishAt`
> (`metadata.youtube.goLiveWhenReady`), and the first run YouTube reports HD
> the worker makes it public (`videos.update`, `privacyStatus: 'public'`,
> status whole) and the row is published at that time. Rejected fails it at
> once; six hours after its upload it is flagged and still waits. A
> breaking release at a time of the owner's choosing is staged as above.
> The dialog says "On YouTube it uploads private and goes public as soon as
> YouTube has HD (often ~30 min)".
>
> **Changes act on YouTube.** Cancelling a staged post (Schedule tab,
> MCP `cancel_scheduled_publish`) deletes its video on YouTube first; a
> refusal keeps the post and says why. A breaking release that moves staged
> Shorts back moves their `publishAt` on YouTube and returns any refusal;
> any other change to a staged post's `scheduled_at` (the cadence moving a
> queue) is carried to YouTube by the next check. Deleting an episode
> (KB-207's trigger) marks its staged YouTube posts `deleting` with
> `metadata.youtube.orphanedAt` instead of deleting them, and the next
> check deletes the video on YouTube, then the row (migration
> `20261022112322_kb214-staged-youtube-orphans.sql`); a staged post whose
> episode was deleted while it uploaded is deleted the same way. Every
> YouTube refusal is returned and kept on the row (`metadata.youtube.error`).
>
> **Where it shows.** The Schedule tab says "Uploaded, goes public at
> <time>", "Held: HD not ready, now <time> (held N times)", highlights a
> row held over six hours, and edits the lead time under Posting rules
> (MCP `update_project` sets `youtubeLeadMinutes` too). The calendar posts
> (`get_publish_calendar`, the episode's publishes) carry `youtube:
> {staged, goLiveWhenReady, publishAt, delays, alert, error}`; the morning
> digest lists held videos under `publishing.youtubeHeld` with a line each;
> the MCP workflow guide explains staging and the gate.

**Severity:** High. Launch is when a video gets most of its first-day
viewers; served 360p, they left, which hurt retention.

**Found:** 2026-10-22, from the owner: viewers arriving at a scheduled
YouTube launch were served 360p.

### What happened

- `YouTubeProvider.uploadVideo` and the worker's YouTube handler set
  `privacyStatus` from the input and never `publishAt` ("scheduling handled
  server-side by cron job"), and the scheduled-publish Lambda picked a row
  only once `scheduled_at <= now`. So every scheduled YouTube video was
  uploaded at its slot and public the moment the upload finished.
- YouTube serves the lowest resolutions first and the HD versions only
  when they are processed, minutes to hours later for a long video.

### Not covered

- **4K.** YouTube's API reports `contentDetails.definition` as `hd` or
  `sd`; nothing says whether the 4K version is ready. A video can go public
  in HD while 4K is still processing. The Schedule tab's lead-time copy
  says so.
- The gate depends on the worker reaching YouTube: if the channel's token
  cannot be used (expired, not refreshed yet), the check waits, and YouTube
  publishes a staged video at its `publishAt` whatever its resolution.
- No edit path changes a scheduled post's title, description or thumbnail,
  so none was carried to YouTube; a staged video's metadata is edited in
  YouTube Studio.
- A post staged but not yet public is not counted toward its channel's
  daily cap when another post of that day reaches the cap check; the
  release planner already places posts within the cap.
- An episode cannot opt into "go public when ready" for normal slots (the
  owner's optional item 3); only a breaking release does.
- The cadence's in-process job (`processScheduledPublishes`, local
  development only) skips staged rows but does not stage: production runs
  the Lambda.
- Screenshot of the Schedule tab: pending; the local stack (Docker) was
  not running, so the rows are covered by a React Testing Library test.

### Acceptance

- [x] A YouTube post due within the project's lead time uploads private
  with `publishAt` at its slot; one within two minutes uploads public at
  its time.
- [x] A staged post is neither staged twice nor sent by the normal path.
- [x] Not HD or still processing ten minutes before: `publishAt` +10 min
  on YouTube and in `scheduled_at`, delay recorded; HD and processed: left.
- [x] Rejected: failed early with YouTube's reason.
- [x] Public on YouTube: published at its go-live time.
- [x] Moved: YouTube's time moves; cancelled: deleted on YouTube; episode
  deleted: the job deletes it on YouTube.
- [x] Held six hours: flagged, still held.
- [x] A breaking release stays private until HD, then goes public.
- [x] The lead time is editable on the Schedule tab and over MCP.
