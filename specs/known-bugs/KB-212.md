## KB-212 — An episode's watch time was its total divided by the number of posts, and each platform's figures were not one metric

> **Fixed (2026-10-20), #691.** Six defects in the analytics figures, found
> in one review of the owner's first week of real data:
>
> 1. **The episode Watch Time card was an average labelled a total.**
>    `getEpisodeAnalytics` summed watch time over the episode's publishes and
>    divided by the number that measured it (`avgWatchTimeSeconds`); the
>    card's tooltip says "Total time viewers spent watching", and MCP
>    `get_episode_analytics` handed the same figure on. A YouTube long with
>    41m and a Facebook Reel with 56m read 48m. It is now
>    `totalWatchTimeSeconds` (the sum; null when nothing measured it, so
>    KB-149 still holds) and `watchTimeByPlatform`, shown under the figure as
>    "YouTube 41m · Facebook 56m". `avgWatchTimeSeconds` is gone; it had no
>    other reader.
> 2. **Facebook watch time switched metric.** `viewTimeMs` was
>    `post_video_view_time` when the optional Reels read answered and
>    `total_video_view_total_time` when it did not. The two count different
>    things (a Reel's includes replays), so a day whose Reels read failed
>    after one that succeeded stored the difference between two metrics as
>    that day's watch time. Each publish now reads one metric by its
>    `content_type` (`short` is a Reel), threaded into the sync as
>    `PublishForSync.content_type`; if that metric is missing the figure is
>    null, never the other.
> 3. **Facebook's average was never fetched.** `total_video_avg_time_watched`
>    (in the video read) and `post_video_avg_time_watched` (in the Reels
>    read) are now requested, by the same rule, and stored in milliseconds as
>    reported on `video_snapshots.fb_avg_watch_time_ms` (ClickHouse migration
>    026; a lifetime quotient, so never a day's delta, as KB-151 decided for
>    Instagram). The morning report reads it as the Facebook video's lifetime
>    `avgViewDurationSeconds` and its retention block; the notes and the data
>    provenance say it is Meta's own per-view average and that a Reel's
>    includes replays.
> 4. **YouTube's audience was the sync window's.** Demographics, geography,
>    devices, operating systems, cities and subscribed status were asked over
>    `computeYouTubeWindow` (last data date − 3 days to now) and stored as the
>    video's audience. They now run from the publish date
>    (`YouTubeAnalyticsInput.publishedAt`) to the end of the window.
> 5. **Silent YouTube queries.** Retention, demographics, device, OS, city and
>    subscribed status each had a bare `catch {}`, so a refusal left no log
>    line and a missing scope or quota error was swallowed. They now go
>    through `optionalQuery`, as traffic and geography did after KB-211: a
>    refusal logs `youtube-analytics.query-refused` with the query, and a
>    scope or quota error fails the sync.
> 6. **Reach CSV zero-fill.** `parseReachReport` read a missing CTR column (or
>    a blank cell) as 0. `video_reach_daily.impressions_ctr` is not Nullable,
>    so a video/day whose impressions or CTR is not a number is now left out
>    rather than written as 0, and a file without the CTR column parses to
>    nothing, which the ingest already logs with the file's header.
>
> Also: two stale comments (Facebook insights "shipped dark", Instagram
> "Graph API v23.0"; the pin is v26.0).
>
> Tests, each red on main's source and green with the fix:
> `episode-analytics-unmeasured.test.ts` "watch time is the total (KB-212)",
> `metric-cards.test.tsx` "watch time split (KB-212)",
> `facebook-watch-time.test.ts`, `youtube-audience-window.test.ts`,
> `csv-parsers.test.ts` (two KB-212 cases) and `daily-figures.test.ts` "a
> Facebook video's average view duration is Meta's, in seconds".

**Severity:** High. The episode page and the MCP tool the morning report's
author reads gave a watch time that was not the figure its label named, for
every episode published on more than one platform; Facebook watch-time days
could be wrong by the difference between two metrics; and every stored
YouTube audience described the last few days rather than the video.

**Found:** 2026-10-20, reviewing the first week of production analytics with
the owner (YouTube and Facebook long videos and Reels on one episode).

**Known limit:** StoryBook's publish worker posts every Facebook video as a
Page video (`isReel: false`); Meta treats a short vertical one as a Reel,
which is why the Reels read answers for Shorts. A Short Meta does not treat
as a Reel now has no watch time (null, said as not measured) rather than the
video metric: that is the rule, not a gap to fill with the other metric.
