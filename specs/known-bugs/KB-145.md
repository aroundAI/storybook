## KB-145 — The LinkedIn connection records the scopes the app requested, not the ones the person granted

> **Fixed (2026-10-01), #TBD.** The callback stores `parseGrantedScopes(tokens.scope)`
> and stamps `metadata.scopes_granted_at`, as the YouTube, TikTok and X
> callbacks do, and records `[]` when LinkedIn sends no `scope` rather than
> the requested list (the rule `parseGrantedScopes` already states). LinkedIn's
> token response carries the grant: "scope — URL-encoded, space-delimited list
> of member permissions your application has requested on behalf of the user"
> (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/authorization-code-flow,
> Step 3, page updated 2026-05-15, read 2026-10-01). The same page says "If
> multiple scopes are requested, the user must consent to all of them and may
> not select the individual scopes", so on real LinkedIn the grant and the
> request should match and the unticked-scope case is the sandbox's; the
> stored value is still the vendor's word rather than ours.
>
> **The class.** Every platform callback was checked for the same mistake:
> YouTube (`callback/youtube` and `youtube/save-channel`), TikTok and X store
> `parseGrantedScopes(tokens.scope)`, and Meta stores the `granted` entries of
> `GET /me/permissions`. LinkedIn was the only one. It was missed because the
> FILM-1711 guard in `packages/features/publishing/__tests__/analytics-scopes.test.ts`
> listed five routes by hand and leaving LinkedIn out, and its pattern
> (`OAUTH_CONFIG|\['`) did not match LinkedIn's `[...scopes]`. The guard now
> reads every `callback/*/route.ts` from disk and requires each stored
> `scopes` to come from a grant reader, so a sixth platform cannot repeat it.
>
> **Downstream.** Stored scopes gate X's upload (`holdsXUploadScope`: an
> `ActionRefusal` from the publish action, the same words as the failed job's
> error in the worker) and analytics (`resolveAnalyticsAccess`:
> `scope_missing`, or `unknown` for no recorded grant); both already treat a
> partial grant as a refusal. Nothing gates on LinkedIn's scopes: a LinkedIn
> publish whose token lacks `w_member_social` fails at LinkedIn, and
> `publishToAll` catches it per platform and returns it as that platform's
> `failed` result, not a thrown action error. Existing LinkedIn rows keep the
> requested list they were stored with until they reconnect.
>
> Red first: the two new callback tests in
> `apps/web/lib/platforms/__tests__/callback-success.test.ts`, the widened
> guard, and the sandbox spec with its `test.fail` removed all failed on the
> unfixed callback (the spec received `w_member_social` after it was
> unticked), and pass with the fix. Mutation guards: `tooling/mutation-guards/kb-145.json`.

**Severity:** Medium. Someone who unticks `w_member_social` on LinkedIn's
consent screen is stored as having granted it, so the app believes it can post
and the first publish fails at LinkedIn with `ACCESS_DENIED` instead of the
Platforms page saying the connection lacks the scope. It does not lose data or
cross accounts, and only a person who narrows the consent screen is affected,
which is why it is not High. **Found:** 2026-10-01, building the LinkedIn
sandbox connect spec (FILM-715).

The callback (`apps/web/app/api/platforms/callback/linkedin/route.ts:164-181`)
stores `LINKEDIN_OAUTH_CONFIG.scopes.personal` (or `.company`) whatever the
token response's `scope` says. The TikTok and Meta callbacks record the grant.

### Reproduced (2026-10-01)

`apps/e2e/tests/platform-connections/sandbox-connect-linkedin.spec.ts:191`
connects through the vendor sandbox, unticks `w_member_social`, and asserts the
stored scopes do not contain it (:206). It is marked
`test.fail(true, 'callback/linkedin records requested, not granted, scopes')`,
so it is reported as an expected failure today; Playwright flags an unexpected
pass when the bug is fixed, and the marker comes off then.

### Proposed fix

Record the `scope` the token response returns (split on spaces), falling back
to the requested set only when the response has none.
